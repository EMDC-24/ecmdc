How we work

Keep the work tied to the decision and the people who will act on it.

We use a simple working method: define the problem, build the evidence, design the answer and support delivery where it is needed.

01

Define the problem

Agree on the real question, who needs to decide, what is in scope and what useful work looks like.

02

Build the evidence

Use data, interviews, documents, field work and workshops to understand what is actually happening.

03

Design the answer

Compare options, test trade-offs and build a recommendation that fits the context.

04

Support delivery

Set up ownership, tracking and problem solving, then transfer the working method to the client team.

How assignments are run

Small teams, clear roles and frequent decisions.

We avoid creating a parallel project universe. The work should fit the client's calendar, decision points and operating reality.

One clear question

We keep the assignment anchored to the decision or result it exists to support.

Direct access to the people who matter

We work with leadership and the teams closest to the issue, not only through project coordinators.

Working sessions, not just presentations

We use workshops to test thinking, make decisions and build ownership while the work is still being shaped.

Useful outputs

Reports matter, but so do decision notes, dashboards, roadmaps, tools, playbooks and operating routines.

When delivery support is needed

Stay involved long enough to make the plan usable.

Some assignments end with a decision. Others need hands-on support while a new program, process or institution settles into place.

A

Delivery setup

Roles, workplans, milestones, reporting and escalation routes.

B

Progress tracking

Simple management information focused on results, risks and decisions.

C

Problem solving

Structured support when implementation hits a bottleneck.

D

Knowledge transfer

Tools, coaching and routines that the client can keep using.

E

Implementation reviews

Independent checks on whether the work is moving as intended.

F

Course correction

Adjusting the plan when evidence or operating conditions change.

Start a conversation

If the problem is not clear yet, that is a valid place to start.

A short conversation can help frame the question, identify the people who need to be involved and decide what kind of work would be useful.
