Institutional reform

Why good reform plans fail during implementation

A strong policy can still fail if ownership, incentives, sequencing and delivery capacity are weak.

ECMDC perspective

Many reform programs are strongest at the point where they are approved. The policy is clear, the presentation is convincing and the expected benefits make sense. Then implementation begins.

That is where the real system appears: overlapping mandates, slow decisions, unclear ownership, budgets that do not match the plan, people who are already carrying full workloads and processes that were not designed for the new way of working.

Design for the institution that exists

A reform plan is more useful when it starts from the institution as it is, not the institution as the plan wishes it to be. That means understanding who controls the decisions, where resources sit, which rules are fixed and which can change, and how work actually moves from one team to another.

A reform is not implemented by the document that describes it. It is implemented by people, budgets, routines and decisions.

Make ownership specific

“The department” cannot own a result. A person or role has to own the next decision, the next milestone and the response when something slips.

Good delivery plans make ownership visible. They also separate three different kinds of responsibility: who decides, who does the work and who needs to be consulted.

Sequence the change

Trying to change everything at once creates noise. A more useful roadmap identifies the few moves that make later changes easier: clarify the mandate, fix a critical process, create a delivery routine, fill a key capability gap, then expand.

Build the management rhythm

Implementation needs a regular forum where progress is reviewed, problems are surfaced and decisions are made. The point is not more reporting. The point is faster resolution of issues that would otherwise sit between teams.

That is often the difference between a reform that remains a plan and one that becomes the new way the institution works.
